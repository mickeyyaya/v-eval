# Research notes: evaluating generated documents, content, and context

Research checked 2026-09-14. This is a selected foundational reading list, not a claim to exhaust the latest literature. Paper dates below distinguish publication dates from first preprint dates. Interpretations and proposed project decisions are marked as recommendations; reported results apply only to the paper's experiment.

## Strongest sources and what each contributes

| Source and date | What it measures / contributes | What it does not establish; implication for v-eval |
|---|---|---|
| [BLEU — Papineni et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P02-1040/), ACL, July 2002 | Modified n-gram precision against reference translations, with a corpus brevity penalty. A historically influential reference-based translation measure; recipient of the NAACL 2018 Test-of-Time award. | Word overlap with a reference is not verification of facts, intent, usefulness, or executable behavior. Recommendation: retain as an optional task-specific baseline, not the default document-quality grade; do not silently treat sentence BLEU as interchangeable with corpus BLEU. |
| [ROUGE — Lin](https://aclanthology.org/W04-1013/), July 2004 | Reference-summary overlap, including n-grams, longest common subsequences, and skip-bigrams. Useful for reproducible summary comparisons when reference summaries exist. [Author's Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/rouge-a-package-for-automatic-evaluation-of-summaries/) explains these variants. | Lexical overlap does not directly measure supported claims or whether a summary enables a user's task. Recommendation: display alongside factuality and requirement coverage, with the exact variant and preprocessing recorded. |
| [BERTScore — Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09675), first preprint 2019-04-21; ICLR 2020 | Token matching using contextual embeddings; supports precision, recall, and F1-like semantic similarity to references and better paraphrase tolerance than exact matching. [Official code](https://github.com/Tiiiger/bert_score). | Similarity is not entailment or verified factual truth. Its encoder, layer, baseline rescaling, language, and reference choice matter. Recommendation: an optional semantic comparison signal, not an acceptance gate for correctness. |
| [FActScore — Min et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/), EMNLP, December 2023 | Splits long-form text into atomic facts and measures the proportion supported by a supplied knowledge source. Provides a retrieval-plus-model estimator. [Paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741.pdf). | Factual precision alone does not measure omitted requirements or completeness. The paper's experiments use biographies and Wikipedia; its reported estimator accuracy must not be presented as a guarantee for other domains. Recommendation: retain claim, source passage, support result, and source snapshot; pair precision with required-information coverage. |
| [ALCE — Gao et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/), EMNLP, December 2023 | Evaluates answer correctness separately from citation recall (statements supported by their citations) and citation precision (citations that contribute support). [Official code/data](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE). | A valid-looking URL is not support; source support is not independent verification of source truth. Automatic NLI judgments can err, including partially supporting redundant citations. Recommendation: evaluate links, support, and missing citations separately, preserving claim-to-passage evidence. |
| [RAGAs — Es et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/), EACL demonstrations, March 2024 | Separates faithfulness to retrieved context, answer relevance to the question, and context relevance. Offers reference-free evaluation signals. [Official repository](https://github.com/explodinggradients/ragas). | Faithfulness is consistency with the supplied context; a faithfully repeated false source can still yield a false answer. Its metric-human agreement varies by dimension and dataset. Recommendation: diagnose retrieval and generation separately; do not market reference-free estimates as ground truth. |
| [ir-measures official measure definitions](https://ir-measur.es/en/latest/measures.html), undated living documentation, checked 2026-09-14 | Recall@k: fraction of known relevant documents retrieved in the top k; MRR: mean reciprocal rank of the first relevant result; nDCG: ranked graded relevance relative to an ideal ranking. Links the original 2002 nDCG paper. | Requires relevance judgments and a defined unit (document, chunk, passage). MRR ignores later relevant items; recall alone ignores ordering; none checks generation quality. Its docs explicitly distinguish Recall@k from Success@k/Hit@k, though some projects use those names interchangeably. Recommendation: pin cutoff, relevance threshold, label version, unjudged-item handling, and nDCG gain/discount implementation. |
| [G-Eval — Liu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153/), EMNLP, December 2023; first preprint 2023-03-29 | Uses a structured evaluation process and form filling with an LLM to assess dimensions such as coherence, consistency, fluency, and relevance. Reported GPT-4 summarization Spearman correlation with human ratings: 0.514. | A moderate correlation in one evaluation setting does not make each numeric grade reliable. The paper discusses bias toward LLM-generated text. Recommendation: use specific, anchored rubrics and validate the judge on representative human-scored cases. |
| [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena — Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), first preprint 2023-06-09; NeurIPS 2023 | Studies single and pairwise judging against human preferences. Identifies position, verbosity, self-enhancement bias, and limited reasoning. Reports over 80% agreement in its setting. [Official evaluation code](https://github.com/lm-sys/FastChat/tree/main/fastchat/llm_judge). | Human preference is not equivalent to requirements compliance or truth, and historical agreement is not transferable to every judge/domain. Recommendation: hide generator identity, swap pair order, test verbosity sensitivity, use reference answers where appropriate, and measure disagreement. |
| [Prometheus 2 — Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01535), first preprint 2024-05-02 | Open evaluator models supporting both direct assessment and pairwise comparison with user-defined criteria. Offers an option for inspectable, controllable evaluation. [Official models/code/data](https://github.com/prometheus-eval/prometheus-eval). | Evaluation-specialized training does not establish universal expertise or eliminate bias. Reported comparisons cover specified evaluation benchmarks and contemporaneous models. Recommendation: make judges replaceable and calibration requirements independent of model/provider. |
| [IFEval — Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911), first preprint 2023-11-14 | Programmatically checks explicit, verifiable instructions, such as word limits and keyword counts. The paper describes 25 instruction types and roughly 500 prompts. [Official implementation](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/instruction_following_eval). | Mechanically satisfying word counts or formatting does not prove that the content is useful or fulfils semantic intent. Recommendation: use deterministic checks for deterministic requirements and preserve separate semantic checks. |
| [LLMBar — Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07641), first preprint 2023-10-11; ICLR 2024 | A meta-evaluation benchmark of 419 response pairs with one instruction-following and one noncompliant output, including deceptive qualities such as engaging tone. Tests the evaluator itself. | A judge can reward a polished response that misses the actual instruction. Success on ordinary preference tests may not transfer to adversarial compliance tests. Recommendation: include attractive-but-wrong counterexamples in v-eval's own calibration suite. |

## Synthesis and proposed design implications

These are recommendations inferred from the sources, not a pre-existing validated universal standard.

1. Make the unit of evaluation a criterion with evidence: user intent → acceptance criterion → artifact span or observation → evaluator result. An overall verdict should follow explicit project policy, while the report preserves separate dimensions. A weighted average must not erase failure of a mandatory criterion.
2. Use three evaluator modes: deterministic predicates for syntax/structure/format; source-backed checks for assertions and citations; calibrated model or human judgment for semantics and usefulness. Prefer stronger direct evidence where available. Passing mechanical checks cannot substitute for missing semantic evidence.
3. Document profile: requirement coverage, factual support, citation quality, internal consistency, audience suitability, clarity, and whether the reader can complete the intended task. Some are judgment dimensions, not established universal numerical indices. Task-specific user outcomes or human evaluation are needed for usefulness.
4. Context profile: relevance to the task, coverage of required information, rank quality where judgments exist, source provenance/freshness, and contradictions. Distinguish the retrieved context from the final answer: good retrieval may be used badly, and a faithful answer can inherit a bad source.
5. Calibrate before gating: have humans independently assess a representative labeled set; retain disagreements and adjudicated labels; include clear passes, clear failures, borderline examples, and persuasive wrong answers. Compare evaluator results with humans per criterion. Report false-accept and false-reject rates, not only agreement, and inspect failure examples. Recalibrate after rubric, prompt, model, or task distribution changes.
6. Measure uncertainty honestly: keep sample count and dataset composition; report variability across runs and disagreement across judges; give confidence intervals for aggregate empirical rates when supported by the sampling design. Do not present a model's self-reported confidence as a calibrated probability. A single-artifact judgment can be inconclusive even when the evaluator performs well on average.
7. Preserve an inconclusive/not-evaluated state. Missing intent, unavailable source evidence, unsupported language, invalid judge output, or missing required execution evidence cannot silently become pass. Distinguish artifact failure from evaluation failure.
8. Make reproducibility visible: record artifact/intent/source hashes or versions, rubric version, evaluator code/model/prompt version, relevant parameters, timestamp, and costs. Public leaderboards compare model systems on their own task distributions; they do not certify this project's particular artifact.

## Suggested short explanation for discussion

The strongest framing is: **“Did this artifact satisfy this intended job, and what evidence supports that decision?”**

Famous indices are useful components, but “meaningful/useful” is contextual. For a cited research memo, a high BERTScore could coexist with fabricated claims. For a concise answer, high factual precision could coexist with omitted required sections. An evidence report that distinguishes those failures is more actionable than an unexplained 87/100 quality score.
